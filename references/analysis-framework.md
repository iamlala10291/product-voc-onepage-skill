# Analysis framework

## The management question

The report must answer: who is entering this product link, what progress they are trying to make, what blocks the decision, where the link loses them, which product values can resolve the block, and what should be changed and measured next.

Do not reduce the answer to “customer service did not ask enough questions.” Customer service may be a handoff weakness, but the diagnosis covers the whole link.

## Five-layer whole-link diagnosis

| Layer | Core question | Typical evidence | Typical action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traffic/content | Did the incoming expectation match the product? | source keywords, creatives, landing behavior | change targeting or expectation-setting |
| Product expression | Did the page explain the value in decision language? | detail page, objections, repeated questions | reorder claims, add proof and use guidance |
| Price/activity | Was the benefit path easy and credible? | coupon delivery/use, activity rules, price comparison | simplify path, clarify thresholds and timing |
| Inquiry handoff | Did the conversation identify user, scene, and proof need? | question sequence, replies, read/no-order behavior | scene discovery and objection modules |
| Fulfillment/after-sale | Did delivery, privacy, packaging, return, or policy create loss? | logistics, refund status, return status, notes | operational/policy correction |

## Fixed narrative

### 01 Management conclusion

Use three layers:

- one-sentence link-level diagnosis;
- three strongest evidence points;
- the priority action sequence.

### 02 Data results and people judgment

Show sample/scope first. Distinguish direct evidence from inference. People judgment should combine:

- lifecycle: visitor, inquirer, buyer, refunder, repeat buyer;
- relationship/usage role: self-use, partner-use, shared use, gifting, first-time exploration, experienced optimization;
- dominant task: solve discomfort, improve atmosphere, simplify preparation, reduce embarrassment, verify safety, seek value;
- decision state: curious, comparing, proof-seeking, price-sensitive, risk-averse, urgent.

Demographics are supporting variables only when observed.

### 03 Empathy map and user tasks

An empathy map is an evidence organizer, not decoration. Fill:

- Who: role and situation
- Need: progress sought and decision to make
- See: competing messages, page content, other users' behavior
- Hear: partner/friend/platform/customer-service messages
- Say: explicit questions and stated objections
- Do: browsing, asking, coupon reading, ordering, refunding, re-ordering
- Think/feel: unspoken fear, embarrassment, anticipation, control
- Pains: reasons to delay or abandon
- Gains: desired functional and emotional outcomes
- Trigger: evidence or experience that moves the decision

Write 6–9 task scenes. Use the full task formula in SKILL.md.

### 04 Three loss paths

#### View-no-buy after coupon

Possible influences include unresolved safety/experience questions, coupon value too weak, unclear use case, price comparison, privacy concern, timing, or the message ending at “coupon sent” without a decision bridge. Treat these as hypotheses until linked to behavior or dialogue.

#### Buy then quick refund

Check mistaken purchase, activity/price re-order, address/privacy concern, specification mismatch, delivery timing, impulse reversal, duplicate order, or policy path. Do not label as product dissatisfaction without use evidence.

#### Refund after shipment

Split logistics intercept, refusal/not received, activity rebate, return without use, and product-experience complaint. Only the last category supports product-experience conclusions.

### 05 Value proof × people opportunity

For each priority task scene, connect:

`concern → verified claim → proof type → purchase reason → response/page placement → metric`

Do not list claims without translating them into the user's decision.

### 06 Action plan

Use three horizons:

- 0–7 days: fix language, page clarification, tracking, and high-risk claims.
- 8–30 days: run scene-based follow-up and page/customer-service experiments.
- 31–60 days: revise product, fulfillment, policy, or acquisition based on measured results.

Each action needs owner, input, output, metric, and stop/continue rule.
